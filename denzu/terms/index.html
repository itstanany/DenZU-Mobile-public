2. What DenZU is, and what it is not

DenZU is a case book: it records the patients, cases, visits, media and notes you enter, and it keeps them in a database on your device. It also carries a clinical content library — departments, fields, options, canal anatomy and media slots — so that a case can be recorded in a consistent shape.

It is not a medical device. It does not diagnose, does not propose treatment, does not calculate doses or drug interactions, and gives no clinical advice. Nothing it shows is a substitute for your own examination, judgement or training, and using the app creates no doctor-patient relationship between us.

The clinical content is a recording vocabulary, not a source of truth about a patient. Where your own clinical judgement and the library disagree, your judgement decides.

4. Your responsibilities

The case book is yours to run, and so are the duties that come with it:

  • Accuracy. What the app holds is what you entered. It is not a second opinion and does not check your entries.
  • Consent and confidentiality. Photographs, audio recordings and clinical notes are personal data about a patient. Obtaining consent for them, and keeping them confidential, is your professional and legal obligation — and your institution's rules on patient records apply as they would to a paper file.
  • Retention and backups. The app stores your cases on the device only. There is no server copy and no account sync, so uninstalling the app, losing the device or clearing its storage removes them. Export anything you need to keep, and follow whatever retention rules apply to you.
  • Device security. Keep the device locked with a passcode or biometrics and keep its operating system updated. The app can be locked, but it cannot protect a device that is already open.
  • Substitution. Where a law, a regulator or your institution requires a certified or approved record, the app is not that record; use it alongside, not instead of.
